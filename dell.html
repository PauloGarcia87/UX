Case Study

Dell Data Mesh

Designing a self-serve platform that helps Dell's engineering domains publish, discover and govern data products — turning a tangle of siloed pipelines into a single, trustworthy data mesh.

Overview

Dell's engineering organization had grown into dozens of independent domain teams, each producing valuable data with no consistent way to share it. Analysts and engineers spent more time hunting for datasets and re‑building pipelines than actually using data to make decisions.

I partnered with a cross-functional squad to design the interface layer of a new internal data mesh platform — the tool every domain team would use to publish, discover and govern their data products.

The Challenge

Through interviews with data owners and consumers across six business units, three recurring pain points surfaced:

  • No shared catalog — teams didn't know which datasets already existed, so duplicate pipelines were common.
  • Zero context on data quality, ownership, or freshness before teams committed to using a dataset.
  • Publishing a new data product required filing tickets with a central platform team, creating weeks of delay.

Process

We ran a series of collaborative workshops to map the end-to-end journey of both data producers and consumers, then translated those flows into low-fidelity wireframes tested with 14 engineers across three sprints.

Key decisions were validated through moderated usability sessions, iterating on the catalog's search and filtering logic, the data product "contract" page, and the self-serve publishing flow.

Solution

The final platform gives every domain team a self-serve console to publish new data products in minutes, complete with ownership, SLAs and schema documentation baked in. Consumers get a searchable catalog with trust signals — freshness, quality score and lineage — visible before they ever query a dataset.

A lightweight approval workflow replaced the old ticketing process, cutting the time to publish a new data product from weeks to hours.
