Case Study

Zoom 360

Redesigning a social learning network where kids learn using Lego toys — turning a barely-used platform with almost no features into an engaging, gamified experience for students, teachers and parents.

7min
Increase in access time, from 40 seconds to a peak of 7 minutes
20k
Increase in the number of users, from 15,000 to 20,000
Role
UX/UI Designer
Audience
Students, teachers & parents
Focus
Engagement & retention

Overview

Zoom 360 is a social learning network for kids to learn using Lego toys. The old design had almost no features, so the average time was just 15 seconds. The platform had the potential for 45 thousand users, but only 15 thousand used it.

Challenge

Redesign and ideate new features for Zoom 360 to increase the number of users and time spent using the platform.

  • Low engagement — the old design had almost no features and sessions lasted only seconds.
  • Untapped potential — thousands of registered users simply weren't coming back to the platform.
  • A sensitive audience — every new feature had to consider the emotional impact on children, especially around competition and rankings.

Process

Meeting with stakeholders to understand the entire context in which Zoom 360 finds itself, the objectives, limitations and collect previously produced data.

Users' journey both of children and teachers. After all, what would be the impact of yet another tool on the lives of teachers who already have a large workload?

Meeting with company's teachers and psychologists to understand the impacts of some ideas presented at the previous meeting on early childhood education and psychology.

Wireframes for ideation and stakeholders approval and prototypes for developers.

Personas were created according to data collected from Zoom to be as assertive as possible when creating the solution.

Data analysis Carrying out usability tests with children is a very big challenge, which requires, in addition to specific skills with the public, parental approval, so we were only able to analyze the data after the changes for evaluation.

Solution

The redesign centered on two pillars: a clearer timeline that surfaces students' work and progress, and a gamification layer that rewards achievement without publicly punishing anyone at the bottom of the ranking.

Timeline

The timeline shows the students' work, and to earn medals they need to take completed challenges. There, teachers also post additional classes, explanations about the challenge and it is also shown whether the student won a medal.

Gamification

One of the concerns regarding gamification was the psychological impact on children who occupy last positions. To overcome this problem, it was decided that the ranking would only show the top 5 placed.
